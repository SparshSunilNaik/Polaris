PROJECT POLARIS DESIGN DECISIONS AND JUSTIFICATIONS
===================================================

1. DETERMINISTIC LAYERED CONTROL
--------------------------------
Problem:
  A learned end-to-end policy would make command provenance and failure
  handling difficult to audit in a safety-oriented student project.

Choice:
  Mission policy, local navigation, active perception, reactive avoidance, and
  final safety are explicit deterministic layers.

Alternative:
  An end-to-end learned navigation/control policy.

Trade-off:
  The selected design is less adaptive but each command, clamp, abort, and
  landing reason is inspectable and testable.

2. SEPARATE FINAL SAFETY GATE
-----------------------------
Problem:
  Avoidance policy and safety enforcement solve different problems.

Choice:
  SafetySupervisor evaluates the already rewritten avoidance decision and is
  the final application component before command transmission.

Alternative:
  Fold all checks into ReactiveAvoidanceController.

Trade-off:
  A separate component adds interfaces, but preserves defense in depth and a
  single auditable SafeMotion boundary.

3. VLM IN SHADOW MODE
----------------------
Problem:
  Semantic models add useful scene descriptions but also latency,
  hallucination, availability, and nondeterminism risks.

Choice:
  VLM, temporal fusion, semantic validation, and Ego-Context observe and report
  after deterministic decisions. They do not enter the safety/control path.

Alternative:
  Let semantic output complete waypoints or choose control actions.

Trade-off:
  This limits semantic autonomy, but permits evaluation without giving an
  optional model authority over flight safety.

4. DETERMINISTIC OBSERVATION COMPLETION
---------------------------------------
Problem:
  Reaching a coordinate is not enough to represent a stable observation, while
  VLM-only completion would be difficult to reproduce.

Choice:
  Gate completion on configured local geometry, heading, health, stability,
  risk, speed, dwell, collision-free state, and fresh depth evidence.

Alternative:
  Position-only completion or VLM/object-detector-only completion.

Trade-off:
  The current method is reproducible but only proves observation conditions at
  a configured target waypoint, not visual recognition or metric range.

5. DISTINCT INFERENCE FRAMES FOR DWELL
--------------------------------------
Problem:
  A 10 Hz control loop can read the same slower depth result multiple times.

Choice:
  Require a new source frame sequence and increasing inference timestamp before
  counting observation evidence.

Alternative:
  Count every control-loop tick spent inside thresholds.

Trade-off:
  Completion latency follows depth inference rate, but stale evidence cannot be
  multiplied into false confidence.

6. BOUNDED YAW-ONLY ACTIVE PERCEPTION
-------------------------------------
Problem:
  Poor texture or glare may require another view, but exploratory translation
  increases collision exposure.

Choice:
  Temporarily use bounded hover/yaw recovery and observation dwell behavior.

Alternative:
  Translational next-best-view planning.

Trade-off:
  Yaw-only recovery is simpler and safer, but cannot solve viewpoints requiring
  a new physical position.

7. LATEST-FRAME BACKGROUND WORKERS
----------------------------------
Problem:
  Depth and VLM inference are slower than camera arrival and can accumulate
  stale queued work.

Choice:
  DepthWorker and VLMWorker retain bounded latest-frame work rather than an
  unbounded FIFO backlog.

Alternative:
  Process every frame in arrival order.

Trade-off:
  Frames may be dropped, but output latency and memory remain bounded and the
  consumer receives more recent evidence.

8. RELATIVE DEPTH AND COARSE RISK GRID
--------------------------------------
Problem:
  The simulator integration needs obstacle response without requiring an
  additional metric depth sensor.

Choice:
  Convert monocular relative pseudo-depth into visual health and a 3x3 risk
  grid for deterministic reactive control.

Alternative:
  Calibrated stereo, LiDAR, dense occupancy mapping, and trajectory planning.

Trade-off:
  The selected path is accessible and computationally bounded, but cannot make
  metric-clearance or map-completeness claims.

9. HEURISTIC BOUNDED REPLANNING
--------------------------------
Problem:
  A failed viewpoint may benefit from one nearby staging objective, while an
  unrestricted planner could violate demo bounds.

Choice:
  Generate at most the configured number of nearby staging waypoints subject
  to offset, duration, path, displacement, and world limits.

Alternative:
  Global graph/search planning over an occupancy map.

Trade-off:
  Bounded insertion is explainable and auditable, but does not prove the path
  to the generated point is globally collision-free.

10. SCOPED TEMPORAL EMERGENCY CONFIRMATION
------------------------------------------
Problem:
  One noisy relative-depth frame can show emergency centre risk during a
  stationary sensing maneuver.

Choice:
  Permit temporal confirmation only for recognized active-perception ownership
  with exactly zero translation. Ordinary translation, collision, stale state,
  and stale required perception fail immediately. Timeout confirms uncertainty.

Alternative:
  Confirm all emergencies temporally, or abort every case immediately.

Trade-off:
  The selected scope avoids delaying ordinary-motion hazards while reducing
  sensitivity to one stationary pseudo-depth sample.

11. ONE-WAY IMMUTABLE EGO-CONTEXT
---------------------------------
Problem:
  A shared writable blackboard can create hidden coupling between semantic
  interpretation and control.

Choice:
  Project runtime state one way into typed semantic context, expose immutable
  snapshots, and retain bounded edge-triggered events.

Alternative:
  Allow all modules to read and write a common blackboard.

Trade-off:
  One-way memory is easier to reason about and audit, but cannot directly adapt
  behavior without a future explicit, safety-reviewed interface.

12. ONE INTEGRATED MAVSDK OWNER
-------------------------------
Problem:
  Multiple command-capable processes could contend for Offboard ownership and
  complicate terminal cleanup.

Choice:
  The integrated mission owns one OwnedMavsdkSystem and the lifecycle of its
  sensor workers, display, collision monitor, landing, and shutdown.

Alternative:
  Separate command, perception, and UI processes with multiple MAVSDK clients.

Trade-off:
  A single owner simplifies authority and cleanup but provides less process
  isolation and is currently a single-vehicle architecture.

13. COMPRESSED JSON ARTIFACTS
-----------------------------
Problem:
  Full image/depth retention is large and unnecessary for every control tick.

Choice:
  Persist sampled states, decisions, transitions, mission outcomes, and
  configuration in JSON.

Alternative:
  Persist synchronized raw RGB, depth maps, PX4 ULog, and simulator logs.

Trade-off:
  Artifacts are compact and replayable, but cannot reconstruct the original
  visual scene or transient semantic results.
