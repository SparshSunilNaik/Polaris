PROJECT POLARIS PROFESSOR QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
===============================================

ARCHITECTURE AND AUTHORITY
--------------------------
1. What can actually command the vehicle?
   The application transmits only SafeMotion returned by SafetySupervisor.
   MAVSDK carries it to PX4, and PX4 retains command acceptance, estimation,
   stabilization, arming, and Offboard behavior.

2. What is the command chain?
   MissionOrchestrator selects policy; LocalGoalNavigator or bounded active
   perception proposes motion; ReactiveAvoidanceController rewrites it;
   SafetySupervisor is the final application gate; _send transmits it.

3. Why is the Safety Supervisor separate from avoidance?
   Avoidance is motion policy; safety is final enforcement. Separation creates
   one auditable boundary and defense in depth.

4. Can the mission orchestrator bypass safety?
   No. It selects objectives and failure policy, but its requested motion still
   passes through avoidance and SafetySupervisor.

5. Can Operator View command the drone?
   The integrated display presents state and accepts display/stop interaction;
   it does not bypass the integrated command chain.

6. Why use one MAVSDK owner?
   It prevents command contention and makes takeoff, landing, cancellation, and
   packaged mavsdk_server cleanup one lifecycle.

7. Is PX4 replaced by Polaris?
   No. Polaris supplies bounded high-level Offboard commands. PX4 remains the
   flight-control and stabilization system.

8. Is the runtime hard real-time?
   No. It is Python asyncio with measured loop timing and fail-closed checks,
   not a real-time scheduling guarantee.

PERCEPTION AND DEPTH
--------------------
9. Is the depth output metric?
   No. It is relative monocular pseudo-depth and must not be described as metres
   to an obstacle or rock.

10. What does the 3x3 grid represent?
    A coarse relative risk summary used by deterministic reactive avoidance.
    It is not a metric occupancy map.

11. Why not process every camera frame?
    A FIFO would accumulate stale inference. Latest-frame workers bound latency
    and memory at the cost of intentionally dropping superseded frames.

12. How is stale perception handled?
    Required stale camera or depth while armed/airborne is an immediate hard
    condition; it does not enter temporal confirmation.

13. Why is visual health separate from depth health?
    Image quality and inference stability expose different failure modes. The
    confidence and safety layers can reject either independently.

14. Does no collision recorded prove the path was clear?
    No. It means CollisionMonitor recorded no unexpected contact. It is not
    independent physical-clearance certification.

OBSERVATION SEMANTICS
---------------------
15. What completes an observation waypoint?
    Configured distance, heading, visual health, depth health/stability, centre
    risk, speed, collision-free state, stable dwell, and fresh depth evidence.

16. Is observation completion object detection?
    No. It proves deterministic conditions near a configured waypoint, not
    recognition of the expected physical object.

17. How do you prevent one depth frame from counting repeatedly?
    ObservationTracker requires a distinct source frame sequence and strictly
    newer inference timestamp before accepting fresh evidence.

18. Why use inference time instead of control-loop time for dwell?
    The 10 Hz loop may consume the same slower depth result repeatedly. Counting
    inference evidence avoids manufacturing confidence from duplicate reads.

19. Why is completion accepted after transmission?
    The implementation requires successful command transmission and no hard
    safety override before accepting completion, so blocked control cannot be
    recorded as safety-approved observation completion.

20. What happens when observation wait expires?
    The tracker resets deterministic observation dwell and returns to positional
    handling; subsequent timeout/failure policy remains with the mission logic.

21. Is the reported observation distance rock range?
    No. It is local-NED distance to the configured waypoint coordinate.

22. Why require low speed during observation?
    It prevents a moving pass from being labelled a stable observation.

ACTIVE PERCEPTION AND REPLANNING
--------------------------------
23. What is active perception here?
    Bounded hover/yaw behavior used to improve a poor view and to own yaw during
    observation dwell.

24. Why is active perception yaw-only?
    It limits collision exposure and planning complexity. The cost is inability
    to solve viewpoints requiring translation.

25. Can active perception ignore emergency risk?
    No. Only zero-translation, recognized active-perception motion may use a
    short temporal confirmation process; timeout confirms and hard-aborts.

26. Is bounded replanning a global planner?
    No. It inserts a limited nearby staging waypoint under configured bounds.
    It does not search a map or prove route clearance.

27. How many replans are allowed in the full mission?
    The current mission and orchestrator configurations allow at most one.

28. What constrains generated waypoints?
    Offset, timeout, mission duration/path/displacement, world bounds, identifier
    uniqueness, and the configured maximum replan count.

SAFETY AND FAILURE HANDLING
---------------------------
29. Which hazards abort immediately?
    Examples include disconnection, invalid/non-finite data, collision, stale
    required state/perception, lost expected Offboard mode, critical confidence
    or depth, control gap, and displacement violation.

30. Why temporally confirm any emergency depth?
    A stationary yaw maneuver can receive one noisy relative-depth frame. The
    exception is narrow, zero-translation, and fails closed on timeout.

31. Does temporal confirmation delay hazards during translation?
    No. Emergency centre risk during ordinary translational motion confirms
    immediately.

32. What happens on a hard override?
    SafeMotion becomes zero with hard_override=true and LAND recommended. The
    waypoint becomes a hard failure and terminal hover/landing follows.

33. What happens on Ctrl+C?
    It records an operator-interruption abort; landing cleanup is shielded from
    cancellation before workers and the MAVSDK owner close.

34. What if command transmission fails?
    The waypoint records a hard failure and does not accept observation
    completion; exception handling attempts controlled landing cleanup.

35. What does confirmed landing mean?
    The application sent the terminal sequence and received fresh landed and
    disarmed telemetry. It is not external ground truth.

36. Why does uncertainty timeout confirm rather than clear?
    Missing fresh evidence cannot prove safety. Failing closed prevents
    indefinite yaw or renewed translation under unresolved emergency risk.

VLM, FUSION, AND EGO-CONTEXT
----------------------------
37. Can the VLM cause waypoint completion?
    No. Deterministic ObservationTracker and mission verification own completion.

38. Can the VLM prevent an abort or clear a collision?
    No. VLM/fusion/Ego-Context values are absent from SafetySupervisor.evaluate.

39. Why include a VLM if it has no authority?
    Shadow mode permits latency, agreement, conflict, and semantic usefulness to
    be evaluated before any safety-reviewed authority is considered.

40. Is VLM enabled in normal mission commands?
    No. It is disabled by default and requires the explicit --vlm flag plus the
    configured Ollama/model infrastructure.

41. What does semantic fusion do?
    It accepts fresh ordered VLM evidence within configured windows and produces
    stable, conflicted, or insufficient fused scene context. It is not Bayesian
    state estimation or field-calibrated probability.

42. What is Ego-Context?
    A one-way typed semantic projection of runtime state with immutable snapshots
    and bounded events. It has no flight-command API.

43. Why immutable snapshots?
    They prevent consumers from mutating shared semantic state and make ownership
    and event edges easier to audit.

44. Are semantic results retained in mission artifacts?
    No. Current schema-version-1 mission JSON omits VLM, fusion, Ego-Context,
    semantic validation, and invocation flags. The final stdout report is
    transient unless separately captured.

EVIDENCE AND RESULTS
--------------------
45. What is the strongest retained successful evidence?
    20260724T205546Z_mission.json records a completed four-waypoint simulator
    mission, deterministic observation completion, one recovery, four avoidance
    transitions, zero recorded collisions, and confirmed landing/disarm.

46. What does the latest retained full-plan artifact show?
    20260725T082730Z_mission.json starts at rock-observation, times out, accepts
    one bounded replan, times out again, aborts, and confirms landing/disarm.

47. Does the no-flight artifact prove a mission completed?
    No. 20260725T082547Z_mission.json records grounded readiness with autonomy
    disabled and no command transmitted; its final label is NO_FLIGHT_COMPLETED.

48. Can the exact original launch commands be recovered from artifacts?
    No. Invocation flags such as --start-waypoint and --vlm are not persisted.

49. Does the successful artifact prove VLM rock recognition?
    No. It predates current expected-target metadata and contains no persisted
    semantic evidence.

50. Does the full mission land on the visible pad?
    No such claim is supported. The final target is explicitly surrogate staging
    before an unvalidated gate, followed by land-at-current-position policy.

51. How many tests pass?
    The current recorded complete result is 285 passing tests. Focused recorded
    counts are 44 for VLM service/worker/fusion/validation and 26 for fusion plus
    semantic validation. These counts are test-suite evidence, not flight proof.

52. Are the artifacts independent proof?
    No. They are useful application-generated evidence and replay input, but are
    unsigned and omit raw synchronized sensors and external ground truth.

53. What is the central contribution?
    A clear safety architecture for combining bounded deterministic autonomy,
    fresh-evidence observation semantics, and optional learned semantic context
    without allowing the learned observer to acquire hidden control authority.

54. What should be done before hardware flight?
    Hardware-in-the-loop testing, calibrated redundant sensing, real-time timing
    analysis, geofencing, independent emergency authority, regulatory/operational
    review, and staged flight tests with an independent safety assessment.
